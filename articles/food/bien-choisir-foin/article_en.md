# Choosing and offering hay

Hay is not a "side dish": it is your rabbit's **most important** food. It should make
up about **85% of the diet**, the rest being greens and a small portion of pellets.

## Why it matters so much

- It wears down the **teeth**, which grow continuously.
- Its **long fibre** keeps the gut moving and protects against digestive shutdowns.
- It keeps your rabbit busy for much of the day.

## How much

**Unlimited hay, at all times**: you can't give too much. As a minimum guide,
provide a **pile of hay at least as big as your rabbit** each day, and refresh it
often so it stays appetising.

## Which hay to choose

- Prefer a **grass hay**: timothy, meadow hay, orchard grass. Mixing types is even
  better.
- **Alfalfa** is too rich in calcium and protein for an adult: save it for **young
  rabbits** and pregnant or nursing females.

## Spotting good hay

- **Green**, with a **sweet**, fresh smell.
- **Dry** and not very dusty.

By contrast, avoid hay that is **mouldy**, **damp** or **strong-smelling**: it can
make your rabbit ill. Store hay somewhere **cool, dry and airy**, out of sunlight.

## How to offer it

Keep it accessible and clean at all times. A trick that works well: place the hay
**near the litter tray** — your rabbit loves to eat and toilet at the same time,
which encourages it to eat more.
