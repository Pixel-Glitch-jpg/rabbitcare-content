# The right leafy greens for your rabbit

Fresh greens are a great complement to hay. Well chosen, they provide water,
vitamins and variety. But **hay remains the foundation**: it should make up most of
the diet, with greens as an addition.

## How much?

How much greens to give depends on your rabbit's **weight**: aim for about **80 to
100 g of greens per kilo of body weight per day**, split into two servings. A few
guidelines by size:

- **Small / dwarf rabbit** (~1.5 kg): about **120 to 150 g** per day.
- **Medium rabbit** (~2.5 kg): about **200 to 250 g** per day.
- **Large rabbit** (~4 kg): about **350 to 400 g** per day.

Offer **at least 3 different types** each day for variety.

## Good choices

- **Herbs**: parsley, coriander, basil, mint, dill, thyme.
- **Leaves**: rocket, carrot tops, radish tops, dandelion, romaine and other
  dark-leaf lettuces (avoid iceberg lettuce, which has little value).
- **Vegetables**: broccoli, celery, courgette, fennel, pepper (in small amounts).

## In moderation

- **Sugary** vegetables (carrot root, pepper, beetroot, parsnip) are **treats**: a
  tiny amount now and then.
- **Cabbage-family** vegetables (kale, broccoli, Brussels sprouts) can cause gas:
  rotate them rather than giving large amounts every day.

## The golden rule: introduce gently

A rabbit's digestion is delicate. For any **new food**:

1. Offer **one new food at a time**, in a small amount.
2. **Wait 24 hours** and check the droppings.
3. If droppings become **soft** or the rabbit has **diarrhoea**, remove that food.

Always keep **unlimited hay**: it is what protects the gut. If you are unsure about
a food, ask a vet who knows rabbits.
