# Your rabbit's teeth: watching for malocclusion

A rabbit has **28 teeth** that grow **all its life**, never stopping — about **2
millimetres a week**. This is normal: chewing fibrous food wears them down as they
grow. The whole balance rests on that.

## Why hay is essential

Chewing **hay** and **grass** takes a long side-to-side movement that **wears the
teeth down evenly**. Pellets and soft vegetables don't wear them enough: a diet low
in hay is the **leading cause** of dental problems. Hence the rule: **unlimited
hay**, at all times.

## What is malocclusion?

When the top and bottom teeth no longer meet correctly, they stop wearing down and
**overgrow**. On the molars, sharp **spurs** form and injure the tongue and cheeks.
Short-faced breeds (lops, dwarfs) are more prone to it.

## Warning signs

- Your rabbit **drools** or has a wet chin.
- It **eats less**, sorts its food, drops it, **loses weight**.
- It has a **runny eye** (an overgrown root can block the tear duct).
- Its **droppings become smaller or rarer**.

Rabbits **hide their pain**: a dental problem often goes unnoticed until it is
advanced. Check the front teeth **once a week** and have a vet check the mouth **at
least once a year**.

## Treatment

Malocclusion cannot be "corrected": there is no brace for rabbits. Overgrown teeth
are **filed down under anaesthetic**, sometimes several times a year. **Never cut
the teeth yourself**: the tooth can split down to the root. At the first sign,
consult a vet who knows rabbits.
