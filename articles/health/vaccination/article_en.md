# Vaccinating your rabbit: myxomatosis and haemorrhagic disease

Two viral diseases threaten pet rabbits, even those living indoors:
**myxomatosis** and **viral haemorrhagic disease (VHD, also called RHD)**. Both are
**often fatal** — and vaccination is exactly what protects against them.

## What you vaccinate against

- **Myxomatosis**: spread mainly by **biting insects** (fleas, mosquitoes). In an
  unvaccinated rabbit it is usually fatal.
- **VHD / RHD**: highly contagious and hardy. There are two strains (**RHD1** and
  **RHD2**). It can kill **with no visible sign** — sometimes the only symptom is
  sudden death.

VHD also spreads via objects, clothes, shoes or contaminated food: an **indoor**
rabbit is therefore not safe either.

## When to vaccinate

- The first vaccination can start from **about 5 weeks**.
- A **booster at least once a year** is needed to maintain protection. In some
  regions, or in high-risk situations, a booster **every 6 months** may be advised.

## A schedule that depends on the country

The available vaccines and the schedule **vary by country** (some vaccines cover
myxomatosis, RHD1 and RHD2 together). In many countries vaccination is not
compulsory but **strongly recommended**.

So the right reflex is simple: **ask your vet** for the schedule suited to your
rabbit and your area. Only a vet can vaccinate, and they will advise the best
timing.
