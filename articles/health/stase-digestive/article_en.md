# GI stasis: a rabbit's number one emergency

Gastrointestinal stasis (a *slowdown or stop of the gut*) is one of the most common
and most serious emergencies in rabbits. The gut slows down and then stops: food
stops moving, and your rabbit's condition can worsen **within hours**.

## Why it happens

Stasis is almost never a stand-alone problem: it is usually the **result** of
something else. The most common causes are:

- **pain** (teeth, gas, a urinary problem);
- **stress** (moving home, travel, losing a companion, loud noise);
- a diet **too low in hay** and too high in pellets, treats or fruit;
- a sudden diet change or a lack of water.

## Warning signs

- Your rabbit **eats little or nothing**.
- Its **droppings become small, rare, or disappear**.
- It is **dull**, sits hunched and still.
- It **grinds its teeth loudly** (a sign of pain) or its belly looks bloated.

## When to act, fast

This is an **emergency**. If your rabbit **stops eating or stops producing
droppings for 12 hours**, contact a rabbit-savvy vet **immediately**. Many
specialists consider that even after **4 hours** without eating you should already
be concerned. A rabbit that isn't eating can decline very quickly: **never wait
until tomorrow**.

## A myth to correct

People often say it's "a hairball blocking the stomach." In reality, the hair is
usually a **consequence** of the slowed gut, not the cause. True prevention means
**plenty of unlimited hay** (the bulk of the diet), water always available, few
treats, exercise, and checking appetite and droppings every day.
