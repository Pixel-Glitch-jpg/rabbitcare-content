# Protecting your rabbit from heatstroke

Rabbits cope **very badly with heat**. They don't sweat and pant poorly: they cool
down mainly through the blood flow in their **large ears**. Heatstroke is a
**life-threatening emergency**, common in summer.

## From what temperature?

A rabbit's comfort zone is around **10 to 20 °C**. **Discomfort** often begins from
**25-28 °C**, and the **danger** rises beyond that. Beware: high **humidity** lowers
that threshold further.

## Recognising heatstroke

- **Fast, shallow** breathing, or open-mouth panting.
- **Red, hot ears** to the touch.
- Lethargy, drooling, disorientation, weakness, trembling.

## Emergency steps

1. **Move your rabbit somewhere cool immediately** (a cool room, the shade).
2. **Cool it down gently**: dampen its **ears** then its body with **cool** (not
   icy) water, or lay a damp towel on it.
3. **NEVER plunge it into cold water**: cooling too suddenly causes shock.
4. **Get to the vet**: heatstroke can be fatal, act without delay.

## Prevention

- Permanent **shade** and good **ventilation** (without blowing directly on the
  rabbit).
- A **frozen water bottle** wrapped in a towel, for it to lie against; cool floor
  tiles work too.
- **Fresh water** always available.
- A **brush** to remove dead fur.

Rabbits that are **old, overweight, long-haired or unwell** are the most fragile:
take extra care during hot spells.
