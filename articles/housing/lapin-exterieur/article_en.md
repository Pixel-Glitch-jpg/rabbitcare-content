# Outdoor rabbits: shelter, weather and safety

A rabbit can live outdoors very well, provided its setup protects it from **cold**,
**heat** and **predators**. Rabbits often cope with cold better than heat — but it is
mainly **sudden changes** in temperature that are dangerous.

## A dry, insulated, well-placed shelter

- **Raise** the hutch off the ground (feet or bricks) to avoid damp.
- Provide a **warm, dry, draught-free** sleeping area, lined with **straw** (straw
  insulates; hay is a food).
- Leave **ventilation openings** to let damp escape.
- Reminder: a hutch alone is not enough — it must be **permanently attached to a
  large run** where the rabbit can move freely.

## Protecting from predators

Foxes, cats, birds of prey, weasels… Safety means:

- **strong welded mesh** (not chicken wire, too flimsy), with a fine gauge,
- fixed **on the inside** of the frame,
- **secure latches** (ideally padlockable): a fox can open a simple hook.

## Through the seasons

- **Summer**: keep the run **in the shade** and watch for **heatstroke** and
  **flystrike** (check the rear end at least twice a day).
- **Winter**: stop the **water freezing**, shelter from wind and rain (part of the
  front can be closed with a clear panel). A **companion** helps a lot: the two
  rabbits keep each other warm.

## Changing environment gently

**Never move** a rabbit used to indoor life straight outdoors (especially into the
cold). Acclimatise it **gradually**, over several days.
