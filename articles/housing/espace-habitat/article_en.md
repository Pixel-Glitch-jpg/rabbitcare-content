# How much space does your rabbit need?

Rabbits need **far more space** than people think. A hutch alone is never enough: at
most it is a place to sleep, not a place to live.

## The reference recommendation

The leading rabbit welfare association (RWAF) recommends, for **two** medium-sized
rabbits, a space of at least:

**3 m × 2 m of floor area, 1 m high** (about 10 ft × 6 ft × 3 ft).

- The floor area must be a **single block of space**, so the rabbit can really
  **run** (not just take a couple of hops).
- The **1 m height** lets it stand up on its back legs and jump.
- These dimensions apply **both indoors and outdoors**.

## "Constant access", not "exercise hours"

The key point: this space must be available **at all times**, day and night, not a
few hours of exercise a day. The ideal is a shelter attached to a large run, where
the rabbit moves around **freely** whenever it wants.

## Good to know

- The **upper level** of a two-storey hutch does **not** count toward the floor
  area.
- Platforms and tunnels don't count either, but they make excellent
  **enrichment**: your rabbit loves to explore, hide and climb.
- Rabbits are **social** animals: they live much better **in pairs** (two neutered,
  properly introduced rabbits). A single rabbit needs the same amount of space.

## Indoors

Avoid slippery floors (tiles, polished wood) that stress the rabbit and harm its
joints: prefer a non-slip floor, a rug or carpet. A mild temperature (around 10 to
20 °C) suits it well.
