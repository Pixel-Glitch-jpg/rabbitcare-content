# Flystrike: protecting your rabbit's rear end

Flystrike (myiasis) is one of the **most serious emergencies** in rabbits,
especially in summer. Flies lay their eggs on the coat, often around the **rear
end**; the **maggots** hatch within **hours** and eat into the flesh. In under **24
hours**, it can become fatal.

## Why it is an absolute emergency

The moment you see **even a single maggot** or a suspicious wound, it is an
**emergency**: take your rabbit to the vet **immediately**, day or night. While
waiting to travel, you can remove visible maggots with tweezers, **without delaying**
the visit, and **without plunging** the rabbit into water.

## Who is most at risk

Any rabbit with a **dirty or damp rear end**: stuck urine and droppings attract
flies. Common causes are:

- **diarrhoea** or soft droppings,
- being **overweight** or having **arthritis**, which stop the rabbit grooming
  itself,
- **dental problems**, old age, a wound.

## Daily prevention

- In warm weather, **check the rear end and under the tail at least twice a day**.
- Keep your rabbit and its home **clean and dry**: remove soiled or damp bedding
  every day.
- Clean and **dry thoroughly** any dirty rear end.
- Treat the underlying **cause** (diarrhoea, teeth, weight) with your vet.
- There are **preventive products** for summer: ask your vet for advice (they stop
  larvae developing but do not treat flystrike that is already present).

Flystrike is frightening, but it is **very preventable** with a daily check during
the warm season.
