# Handling and carrying your rabbit safely

A rabbit must be carried with care. Lifted badly, it can panic and struggle — and a
violent kick can even **fracture its spine**. A few simple habits keep it safe.

## The right way to lift

- Slide **one hand under the chest** (just behind the front legs) and **the other
  under the rear end**.
- **Always support the rear end**: this is the most important point.
- Bring it **against you**, all four feet tucked in, rather than letting it dangle.
- Stay **close to the ground** so that any fall is harmless, and set it down gently.

## What you must NEVER do

- **Never lift it by the ears, the scruff, the legs or the tail**: it is painful and
  dangerous.
- **Never put it on its back.** People often think a flipped rabbit is "relaxed": in
  reality it **freezes with fear** (playing dead). That is not calm, it is
  **distress**.
- Avoid **slippery** surfaces, where the rabbit can injure itself.

## Why so much care

The back-leg muscles are very powerful, while the spine is **fragile**. If a badly
held rabbit gives a big kick, it can **break its back** and be left paralysed. Hence
the importance of always supporting the rear end.

## Taming gently

Get your rabbit used to contact **gradually**: come down to its level, let it
approach, stroke it to settle it before carrying. **Always supervise children**:
only an adult or a responsible older child should carry the rabbit. Many rabbits
actually prefer to be stroked **on the floor** rather than in your arms.
