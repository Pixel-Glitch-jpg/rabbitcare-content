# Moulting and brushing: preventing hairballs

Your rabbit **sheds its fur** regularly: it moults roughly **every 3 months**, with
two big moults a year (in spring and autumn). Indoor rabbits, because of central
heating, sometimes moult almost all year round.

## Why brushing matters

While grooming, a rabbit **swallows its dead fur**. But unlike a cat, a **rabbit
cannot vomit**: everything it swallows must travel through the whole gut. During the
moult, **brushing** removes as much dead fur as possible **before** it is swallowed.
It is a habit that protects digestion.

## How often

- **During the moult**: brush **every day**, sometimes several times a day.
- **The rest of the time**: a weekly brush keeps the coat healthy and builds the
  habit.

Prefer **several short, gentle sessions** over one long one, to avoid stress.

## The right tools

Use a **wide-tooth comb** or a **soft brush** (blunt pins or rubber) and detangle
gently. **Never cut a knot with scissors**: a rabbit's skin is very thin and tears
easily, leave large mats to a vet.

## Hay, water… and watching

During the moult, **unlimited hay and water** are essential: they keep the gut
moving to clear the swallowed fur. Droppings linked together by strands of fur are
**normal** as long as the rabbit is eating well. However, droppings that become
**small or rare**, or a dull rabbit, mean you should seek help **urgently**.

## Never bath your rabbit

A bath is **dangerous**: intense stress, risk of shock, and the rabbit dries very
slowly (risk of hypothermia). If an area is dirty, spot-clean it with a damp cloth,
then dry it well. A full bath should only be done **on a vet's advice**.
