# Nips, thumps and growls: understanding your rabbit

Rabbits don't meow or bark, but they **communicate a lot** — through their posture,
their sounds and their gestures. Learning to read them means respecting them better.

## The signals and what they mean

- **Thumping a back foot**: a **warning** signal. Your rabbit is scared or senses
  danger. Look for what is worrying it and reassure it.
- **Growling**: it feels threatened or is defending its territory. It is saying
  "stop", not "I hate you". **Back off gently** and try again later.
- **"Boxing" stance** (up on the hind legs, front paws raised): clear displeasure, a
  last warning.
- **Loud tooth grinding**: a sign of **pain** or illness. If it is sudden,
  especially with refusal to eat or dullness, see a vet **quickly**.
- **Soft tooth purring** (a light chattering): the opposite — a sign of **contentment**,
  often when you stroke a relaxed rabbit.
- **Binky** (a joyful leap with a twist in the air): **pure joy**. A great sign!

## Does it really bite?

A **gentle nip** is not a bite: it is often a way to get your attention or ask you
to move. A **real bite** usually comes from fear, surprise, being handled in a way
it dislikes, or a finger mistaken for food (rabbits see poorly up close).

## The key rule

**Never punish your rabbit physically**: it doesn't work and it makes the rabbit
fearful. Choose patience, consistency and rewards instead. Many troublesome
behaviours also ease noticeably after **neutering**.
