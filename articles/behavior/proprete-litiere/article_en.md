# Litter-training your rabbit

Good news: rabbits are naturally clean. They instinctively choose one or two corners
for their business. With a little method, a rabbit learns to use a litter tray
easily.

## Secret number one: neutering

At puberty (around 3 to 6 months), an unneutered rabbit becomes **territorial** and
starts marking its space with urine and droppings. A **neutered or spayed** rabbit
is far easier to litter-train. This is by far the most important factor.

## The method, step by step

1. **Watch** which corner your rabbit naturally uses.
2. **Place the tray in that corner.** Choose one big enough for the rabbit to turn
   around in, with a low entry side.
3. **Put a few of its droppings in it** at first: the smell encourages it to return.
4. **Place hay in or right next to the tray**: rabbits love to eat and toilet at the
   same time. This is a very effective trick.
5. **Start in a small space**, then enlarge the area once the tray is used reliably.
6. After an accident, **clean with white vinegar** (or an enzyme cleaner), never
   bleach, and **never scold** your rabbit.

## Which litter?

- **Safe**: recycled paper pellets, kiln-dried wood pellets, hemp, flax, dust-free
  straw — topped with fresh hay.
- **Strictly avoid**: **clumping cat litter** (it swells if swallowed and can block
  the gut) and **pine or cedar shavings** (their fumes irritate the airways).

## A sudden change

If a clean rabbit suddenly starts going everywhere, it can signal a health problem
(such as a urinary infection). In that case, see a vet who knows rabbits.
