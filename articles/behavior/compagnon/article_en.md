# Your rabbit needs a companion

Rabbits are **very social** animals. In the wild they live in groups and rely on
each other for warmth and safety. Living **alone** weighs on them: boredom, stress,
sometimes signs of distress. The best company for a rabbit is… **another rabbit**.

## The right pair

- Keep your rabbits **at least in pairs**.
- The easiest pair to form is often a **neutered male + a spayed female**.
- **Neutering both is essential**: without it, hormones cause fighting and breeding.
  **Never** put two unneutered rabbits together.

## What about a guinea pig?

Contrary to an old myth, a guinea pig is **not** a good companion for a rabbit: they
don't understand each other, the rabbit can injure it, their dietary needs differ,
and the rabbit can pass on a dangerous bacterium. Each is happier with its own
species.

## Forming the pair: patience first

Introductions ("bonding") are done **gradually** and on **neutral ground**, a place
where neither rabbit has lived before. A few pointers:

- Work in short, daily sessions, supervised.
- **Chasing and mounting** at first are **normal** (establishing the pecking order):
  only separate if there is a real fight.
- The time it takes **varies widely**: from one afternoon to several weeks. The key
  word is patience.

If you adopt a second rabbit, have both neutered and wait **a few weeks** after the
operation before starting the bonding. If in doubt, a vet or a rabbit charity can
help you.
